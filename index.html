Selected work · Impact study

Learning to read a city as a system.

We designed a field-based course, published its results, and then measured what it changed in how students think. This is the method we bring to every learning programme.

Setting
B.Des Interaction Design course, Delhi Technological University
Published

    Students went into Purani Dilli's markets with 13 asks: use every sense, talk to people, and work out how the place runs.

    Their essays, sketches, maps and sensory journey charts became a full journal issue.

    Each essay was coded for learning, perception and depth of thinking against the brief.

How we measured it, and its limits

Akanshya Dutta coded the published essays against the field brief for learning, perception and cognition. The evidence is what students chose to write, edited for publication, and there was no measurement before the visit. It shows that learning happened, not how much.

What changes next time

Short reflections before and a week after the visit, one recorded conversation per student, and the five-step ladder named in the brief. That turns self-report into measured change, and it is the same design we use for organisations and for Drishta's retreats.

How we work

Four stages, in this order.

We spend real time on the first two stages, so that the design solves the right problem.

  1. Listen

    We meet you and the people the work is for, and learn how things actually run today.

  2. Frame

    We agree in writing what the problem is, who it is for, and how we will know it worked.

  3. Make

    We design, share drafts early, and revise with you rather than presenting a finished surprise.

  4. Hand over

    You get finished files and the guidance to use and maintain them without us.
