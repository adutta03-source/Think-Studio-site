The framework

Metacognition Impact Framework

Six dimensions, each assessed on a common 0–4 scale and grounded in an established, peer-reviewed instrument, so that individual and cohort profiles can be tracked over time.

  • Foundational
  • Metacognitive
  • Transfer
  1. NoticeBreadth and quality of multisensory attentionIngold; sensewalking
  2. ThinkDepth of reasoning and synthesisSOLO taxonomy
  3. KnowKnowledge of one's own cognitionFlavell; MAI
  4. SteerPlanning, monitoring and self-regulationZimmerman; MAI
  5. ReflectCritical examination of assumptionsKember; Mezirow
  6. CarrySustained transfer into practiceKolb

Four-point measurement protocol

Approximately 60 minutes of participant time across four touchpoints, applicable to academic field programmes and residential retreats alike.

Qualitative evidence

  1. From disorder to systema carefully crafted system based on frugality, improvisation, memoryThink
  2. Multisensory observationSmell used as a design instrument: spent tea leaves to deter loitering, kettle steam to draw customers.Notice · paraphrased
  3. Attention to the overlookedoverlooked precisely because he is constantNotice
  4. Reflexive awareness of methodphotography flattens… eliminates sound, neutralizes smellReflect

Excerpts from student essays, Design for All, Vol 21 No 2. Contributors are anonymised.

Evidence standard

  1. Baseline and follow-up. Pre- and post-programme scores for every participant.
  2. Independent scoring. Two raters, blind to timepoint, with inter-rater agreement of κ ≥ 0.6.
  3. Practical significance. A minimum gain of one level, reported with effect sizes.
  4. Durability. At least 50% of the gain retained at three months.

Limitations

Scores were assigned retrospectively by a single rater from published, edited texts, without a baseline. They demonstrate the framework's application rather than a causal effect. The full protocol, including a comparison group, will be applied from October 2026 in Drishta's programme and Think Studio's next university cohort.

03 Repeatable programme modelDesign Mentorship, Delhi Technological UniversityThree-week immersion · Two editions, 2025 and 2026 · Programme lead: Dr. Partha Pratim Das 2,086+applicants88%completion

An intensive three-week programme building foundational design capability among school students and early-stage undergraduates from all disciplines. Programme lead: Dr. Partha Pratim Das, Faculty Coordinator, Department of Design, Delhi Technological University. Delivered with the department's design society, Tetrix.

Edition 1202530 June to 21 July
  • 986+registrations
  • 180participants
  • 158completions (88%)
  • 9industry sessions
  • Structure. Three parallel tracks of 60 participants: UX, visual and product design.
  • Pedagogy. Mentor-led instruction four days a week, each session paired with an applied task, plus a weekly industry practitioner session.
  • Cohort. School students and first-year undergraduates from all disciplines, selected through a national open call.
  • Output. All 158 graduates presented a completed project and portfolio at a public exhibition.
Edition 220266 to 27 July
  • 1,100+registrations
  • 180participants
  • 3 × 3tracks × weeks
  • 9industry sessions
  • Advanced curriculum. Each track progressed from fundamentals to advanced practice: service and system design (UX), motion and sound (visual), prototyping and manufacture (product).
  • Industry exposure. First industrial site visit, product teardowns and 3D printing.
  • Emerging tools. AI-assisted rapid prototyping and narrative-led pitching.
  • Expert evaluation. Team presentations to an external jury in a professional review format.
Outcomes and implications
  • Validated demand. 2,086+ applications for 360 places across two editions (approximately 6:1), with 12% year-on-year growth.
  • Repeatable model. Delivered twice at full capacity; the second edition increased depth without increasing scale.
  • Strong retention. 88% completion in the first edition.
  • Measurement opportunity. Current reporting captures participation and output. Applying the Metacognition Impact Framework would quantify cognitive change across a cohort of 180.
  • Client application. Think Studio adapts and delivers this model, with built-in evaluation, for universities, schools and corporate teams.
